0 · Notation

\(N\) names, indexed \(i\); \(T\) daily observations, indexed \(t\). \(r_{i,t}\) is a simple daily return. \(\Sigma\) is the annualised covariance of the names and \(\sigma_i = \sqrt{\Sigma_{ii}}\). \(\mu\), \(\pi\) and \(Q\) are annualised excess returns over the risk-free rate \(r_f\); mixing an excess prior with a total-return view would bias every high-beta name, so the units are enforced at the seam. \(w\) is a weight vector that sums to one. \(\Phi^{-1}\) is the standard normal quantile. A bold-face live value beside an equation is the number the last run used.

1 · Signals

Four cross-sectional scores are computed for every name at every run. They are published as diagnostics and do not currently reach the weights: the view source is —, so the Black-Litterman views in §4 come from the valuation model. This section is stated in full because the machinery is live, its information coefficients are being measured, and it is the path momentum takes if it earns one.

Sector neutralisation, then a Gaussian rank

Each raw metric \(x_i\) is partially demeaned within its sector \(s\) of \(n_s\) covered names, shrunk so a one-name sector keeps its value:

\[ \tilde{x}_i \;=\; x_i \;-\; \frac{n_s - 1}{n_s}\,\bar{x}_s \]

then ranked across the whole universe and mapped to a normal score rather than z-scored, because with thirty names a raw z-score is owned by its outliers:

\[ s_i \;=\; \Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{rank}(\tilde{x}_i) - \tfrac12}{N_{\text{valid}}}\right) \]

A missing metric scores \(0\), neutral, rather than an invented extreme. Fewer than three valid names and the whole score is undefined.

The four scores

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{value}_i &= \operatorname{mean}\big\{\, s(1/\text{PE}),\; s(1/\text{EV/EBITDA}),\; s(\text{FCF}/\text{cap}) \,\big\} \\ \text{quality}_i &= \operatorname{mean}\big\{\, s(\bar{\rho}/(\sigma_\rho + 0.01)),\; s(\text{op.\,margin}),\; s(\text{ROE}),\; s(-\text{D/E}) \,\big\} \\ \text{momentum}_i &= s\!\left(\textstyle\prod_{t=T-252}^{T-22}(1 + r_{i,t}) - 1\right) \\ \text{short}_i &= -\,s(\text{short \% of float}) \end{aligned} \]

where \(\rho\) is quarterly return on invested capital over at least three quarters, and the momentum window is the prior twelve months skipping the most recent one (§2 is about that choice). The mean over sub-metrics skips the ones a name lacks: a bank with no EV/EBITDA, no free cash flow and no leverage figure is scored on earnings yield alone, and the run says so.

The composite and its weights

\[ c_i \;=\; \sum_{k} w_k\, s_{i,k}, \qquad w = \big(\text{value } \mathbf{\text{⟪m-w-value|—⟫}},\; \text{quality } \mathbf{\text{⟪m-w-quality|—⟫}},\; \text{momentum } \mathbf{\text{⟪m-w-mom|—⟫}},\; \text{short } \mathbf{\text{⟪m-w-short|—⟫}}\big) \]

Those weights are static until every signal has 6 point-in-time snapshots behind it, after which they switch to the measured information coefficients:

\[ w_k \;=\; \frac{\max(\mathrm{IC}_k, 0)}{\sum_j \max(\mathrm{IC}_j, 0)}, \qquad \mathrm{IC}_k \;=\; \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} \rho_{\text{Spearman}}\!\big(s_{\cdot,k}(t_m),\; r_{\cdot}(t_m \to t_m + 21)\big) \]

Live: —. Measured so far — momentum —, value —, quality —, short interest —, on — snapshots. With thirty names and \(M\) periods the standard error of a null IC is about \(1/\sqrt{29M}\), which is the reason the momentum question is settled on a wider panel in §2 rather than here.

Grinold's rule, if a signal were a view

The composite is re-standardised to \(z_i\) across the tradeable names and scaled by an assumed coefficient and the name's forecast volatility:

\[ \alpha_i \;=\; \mathrm{IC}\cdot \hat{\sigma}_i \cdot z_i, \qquad \mathrm{IC} = \mathbf{\text{⟪m-ic-assumed|0.05⟫}} \]

and the signal view would be \(Q^{\text{sig}}_i = \pi_i + \alpha_i\). The coefficient is an assumption until the table above can replace it. Nothing in the live book uses this today.

2 · Momentum, tested

Momentum is one word for several distinct claims about where the information in past returns lives. Each is written below as the literature specifies it; the table then reports how each performed on a point-in-time panel wide enough to say, graded at the bar every other claim in this system has to clear: \(|t| \ge 3\).

Definitions

With \(R_i(a, b) = \prod_{t=T-a}^{T-b-1}(1 + r_{i,t}) - 1\) the return over the window ending \(b\) days before now:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{12-1 (Jegadeesh–Titman; Carhart)} &:\quad R_i(252, 21) \\ \text{12-7 intermediate (Novy-Marx)} &:\quad R_i(252, 126) \\ \text{6-1 recent} &:\quad R_i(126, 21) \\ \text{volatility-scaled (Barroso–Santa-Clara)} &:\quad R_i(252, 21)\,\big/\,\hat{\sigma}_{i,126} \\ \text{residual (Blitz–Huij–Martens)} &:\quad \frac{\sum_{t \in \text{12-1}} \varepsilon_{i,t}}{\operatorname{sd}(\varepsilon_{i,\cdot})}, \quad \varepsilon = r_i - \hat{a} - \hat{\beta}^{\!\top} F \text{ over 36 months} \\ \text{52-week high (George–Hwang)} &:\quad P_{i,T} \,\big/\, \max_{t \in [T-252,\,T]} P_{i,t} \\ \text{information discreteness (Da–Gurun–Warachka)} &:\quad \operatorname{sign}(R_i)\,\big(\%\text{down} - \%\text{up}\big) \\ \text{one-month reversal (Jegadeesh 1990), a control} &:\quad -R_i(21, 0) \end{aligned} \]

\(F\) is the Fama–French market, size and value factors; \(\hat{\sigma}_{i,126}\) is the name's own six-month realised volatility. The reversal row is not a momentum signal — it is the reason every other row skips a month, and it is tested so the skip can be seen to be earned. Three precisions from the primary texts: Jegadeesh and Titman's original skip was a week; the one-month skip is Carhart's and Fama–French's convention. The residual here is taken after the intercept, so a name's steady three-year alpha does not count as momentum; the older residual signal in the point-in-time panel keeps the intercept in, and the two are reported side by side. And Daniel and Moskowitz show momentum's crashes are a short-side phenomenon — the losers that rally — which a long-only book is spared, at the cost of holding low-beta winners into the rebound.

3 · The prior

Market weights are capitalisation weights within the tradeable universe, a missing cap replaced by the median. The risk aversion is not estimated from realised means — the textbook estimator inverted the cross-section on this window — but pinned by the identity that the market portfolio must earn the equity risk premium:

\[ \lambda \;=\; \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{\beta_u\,\mathrm{ERP}}{w_{\text{mkt}}^{\!\top}\Sigma\, w_{\text{mkt}}},\; 0.3,\; 10\right), \qquad \beta_u = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(r_u, r_m)}{\operatorname{var}(r_m)}, \quad r_u = w_{\text{mkt}}^{\!\top} r \] \[ \pi \;=\; \lambda\,\Sigma\,w_{\text{mkt}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad w_{\text{mkt}}^{\!\top}\pi = \beta_u\,\mathrm{ERP} \]

Live: \(\lambda\) = —, \(\beta_u\) = —, ERP = —, the one expected-return number in the system that is asserted rather than estimated.

4 · Views and their uncertainty

A view is the valuation model's implied return — the discount rate at which a ten-year cash-flow model equals today's price — converted to excess units, with an uncertainty read from the model's own bear-to-bull spread and floored:

\[ Q_i \;=\; \text{implied}_i - r_f, \qquad \text{spread}_i = \tfrac12\,\big|\text{implied}^{\text{bull}}_i - \text{implied}^{\text{bear}}_i\big|, \qquad \omega^{\text{raw}}_i = \max(\text{spread}_i,\; \mathbf{\text{⟪m-floor|0.03⟫}})^2 \]

The raw uncertainties are then rescaled so the views and the prior get the say a stated confidence \(c\) implies. Before this rescaling the posterior correlated 0.93 with the views and 0.36 with the prior — the prior was decorative.

\[ \Omega_{ii} \;=\; \tau\,\Sigma_{ii}\cdot\frac{1-c}{c}\cdot\frac{\omega^{\text{raw}}_i}{\operatorname{mean}_j\,\omega^{\text{raw}}_j}, \qquad c = \mathbf{\text{⟪m-conf|0.50⟫}}, \quad \tau = \mathbf{\text{⟪m-tau|0.05⟫}} \]

At \(c = \tfrac12\) the middle factor is one and each view's variance is \(\tau\Sigma_{ii}\) scaled by its relative spread. The Idzorek reading — that the posterior lands \(c\) of the way from prior to view — is exact for one view with unit relative spread and approximate otherwise. Live: — views; prior–posterior correlation —. If the view source were blend, the view would be \(0.7\,Q^{\text{dcf}} + 0.3\,Q^{\text{sig}}\).

5 · Black–Litterman

With \(P\) the selection matrix that picks out the names carrying a view (absolute views, one row each; a name without a view stays at its prior), the posterior expected excess return is

\[ \mu \;=\; \Big[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1} + P^{\!\top}\Omega^{-1}P\Big]^{-1}\Big[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1}\pi + P^{\!\top}\Omega^{-1}Q\Big] \]

solved directly when the system is well conditioned and by pseudo-inverse otherwise. This \(\mu\) is the optimizer's input. The optimizer treats it as certain; the robustness layer that would stop that is the largest piece not built.

6 · Covariance

A statistical factor model, checked against shrinkage

On the trailing 252 days of demeaned daily returns \(X\), the sample covariance \(S = X^{\!\top}X/(T-1)\) is decomposed and the top \(k =\) 3 eigencomponents kept:

\[ \Sigma_{\text{daily}} \;=\; B\,F\,B^{\!\top} + D, \qquad D = \operatorname{diag}\!\big(\max(\operatorname{diag}(S - BFB^{\!\top}),\; 10^{-8})\big), \qquad \Sigma = 252\,\Sigma_{\text{daily}} \]

Alongside it, every run, a Ledoit–Wolf estimate toward the constant-correlation target \(F_{ij} = \bar{r}\sqrt{s_{ii}s_{jj}}\):

\[ \Sigma_{\text{LW}} \;=\; 252\,\big[\delta F + (1-\delta) S\big], \qquad \delta = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{\hat{\pi} - \hat{\rho}}{\hat{\gamma}\,T},\; 0,\; 1\right) \]

with \(\hat\pi, \hat\rho, \hat\gamma\) the Ledoit–Wolf estimators of the shrinkage constant. It is a cross-check: if the two disagree on equal-weight volatility by more than 20% the run says so. Live shrinkage intensity —; equal-weight vol — (factor) against — (shrunk).

Correlations long, volatility short

The diagonal is then replaced by a forecast while the correlation structure is kept, because the two move at different speeds:

\[ C \;=\; \operatorname{diag}(\sigma)^{-1}\,\Sigma\,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)^{-1}, \qquad \Sigma' \;=\; \operatorname{diag}(\hat{\sigma})\; C \;\operatorname{diag}(\hat{\sigma}), \qquad \Sigma' \leftarrow \tfrac12(\Sigma' + \Sigma'^{\top}) \]

A name without a forecast keeps its model volatility. Trailing one-year realised volatility is published beside \(\hat\sigma\) but never used in its place.

7 · The volatility forecast

Each name's return history is demeaned and an exponentially weighted variance taken with the most recent bar at \(k = 0\), weights renormalised over the finite window:

\[ \hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{daily}} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{k \ge 0} (1-\lambda)\lambda^{k}\, r_{T-k}^{2}}{\sum_{k \ge 0} (1-\lambda)\lambda^{k}}, \qquad \hat{\sigma} = \sqrt{252\,\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{daily}}}, \qquad \lambda = \mathbf{\text{⟪m-ewma-lambda|0.94⟫}} \]

The half-life at that decay is about eleven days and 99% of the weight sits in the last 75 bars, so more than a year of history is already more than the estimator can use. Below 60 observations the forecast is undefined.

A GARCH(1,1) alternative is fitted by Gaussian likelihood on standardised returns, \(\sigma^2_t = \omega + \alpha r^2_{t-1} + \beta\sigma^2_{t-1}\) with \(\omega > 0,\ \alpha, \beta \ge 0,\ \alpha + \beta < 1\), and forecast one step ahead. It is built and inactive. So is a blend with implied volatility, \(0.6\,\hat\sigma + 0.4\max(\mathrm{IV}_{30} - 0.02,\, 10^{-6})\), waiting on an option-surface history long enough to trust.

8 · The optimizer

\[ \max_{w}\;\; \mu^{\!\top}w \;-\; \lambda_{\text{opt}}\, w^{\!\top}\Sigma' w \;-\; \gamma\,\lVert w - w_0 \rVert_1 \] \[ \begin{aligned} \text{subject to}\quad & \textstyle\sum_i w_i = 1, \qquad w \ge 0 \\ & w_i \le \min(\text{cap}_i,\; \text{fixed}_i), \qquad \text{cap}_i = \mathbf{\text{⟪m-name-cap|12.5%⟫}} \text{ unless overridden} \\ & w_i \ge \text{floor}_i \quad\text{for every fixed or floored name} \\ & \textstyle\sum_{i \in \text{cluster}} w_i \le \text{cap}_{\text{cluster}}, \qquad \textstyle\sum_{i \in \text{theme}} w_i \le 0.35, \qquad \textstyle\sum_{i \in \text{sector}} w_i \le \text{cap}_{\text{sector}} \\ & \operatorname{sum\_largest}(w,\; \mathbf{\text{⟪m-min-pos|8⟫}} - 1) \le 1 - 0.02 \qquad \text{(at least eight names, convexly)} \end{aligned} \]

\(w_0\) is the last recorded book drifted to today; \(\gamma\) = 0.0002 is a one-way transaction-cost coefficient amortised over a five-year horizon. A fixed weight is a floor and a cap at the same number. Cluster caps in force: ⟪m-cluster-caps|—⟫.

The multiplier is not chosen

\(\lambda_{\text{opt}}\) is bisected on \(10^{m},\ m \in [-2, 5]\), until the solution's volatility \(\sqrt{w^{\!\top}\Sigma' w}\) lands inside the band, stopping at the first inside value. The band is the actual risk statement; the multiplier is its Lagrange multiplier. If even the minimum-variance portfolio sits above the ceiling the band is infeasible and the target becomes \(1.02\times\) that minimum. Live: model vol — in a band of —.

Two passes

Pass one solves relaxed over every name. The support is \(\{i : w_i \ge 0.02\}\) plus every protected name; pass two re-solves on that support with \(w_i \ge\) 2% as a lower bound, so dust cannot exist rather than being cleaned up afterwards. If a non-protected name lands exactly on the floor, the lowest-\(\mu\) such name is dropped and the pass repeats, never below eight names. Weights under \(10^{-4}\) are zeroed and the result renormalised.

9 · When it trades

Solving and trading are separate decisions. With \(t\) the target and \(c\) the held book:

Hard triggers, any time

A held weight over its cap by more than 3pp; an excluded name held above 2%; fewer than eight positions above 2%; or volatility above the ceiling 22%.

Soft triggers, after 80 days

\[ \text{band breach}_i \;:\; |t_i - c_i| \ge \mathbf{\text{⟪m-band-abs|0.02⟫}} \;\;\text{and}\;\; \frac{|t_i - c_i|}{t_i} \ge \mathbf{\text{⟪m-band-rel|0.25⟫}} \] \[ \mathrm{CE}(w) = \mu^{\!\top}w - \lambda\, w^{\!\top}\Sigma' w, \qquad \text{fire if}\;\; 10^4\big[\mathrm{CE}(t) - \mathrm{CE}(c)\big] \;>\; 2\,\text{cost}_{\text{bp}} \;\;\text{and}\;\; \ge \mathbf{\text{⟪m-ce-floor|25⟫}}\,\text{bp} \] \[ \text{cost}_{\text{bp}} \;=\; \tfrac12 \textstyle\sum_i |t_i - c_i| \times \mathbf{\text{⟪m-spread-bp|8⟫}} \]

The certainty-equivalent test uses the investor's \(\lambda\) from §3, not the optimizer's bisected multiplier — using the latter once reported a 1,594bp loss for a trade whose entire content was the vol band doing its job. A confirmed change in the narrative regime is the third soft trigger.

The move

\[ w_i \;=\; c_i + \mathbf{\text{⟪m-adjust|0.30⟫}}\,(t_i - c_i) \quad\text{for names outside their band; others untouched} \]

then floored at zero, excluded names zeroed, renormalised, and any name over its cap pushed inside it. One thing to know about that equation: it is what the report shows. The holdings history records the full target \(t\), not the staged \(w\), so the partial move is a recommendation to the owner rather than a fact about the recorded book.

10 · The regime switch

A two-state Markov-switching model with switching variance is fitted to twenty years of weekly index returns, and the filtered probability of the high-variance state at the last week, \(p\), sets the band:

\[ \text{band} \;=\; \begin{cases} \mathbf{\text{⟪m-band-stress|12–15%⟫}} & p \ge 0.5 \\ \mathbf{\text{⟪m-band-calm|19–22%⟫}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

It is a threshold, not a continuous scaling, and it only ever scales exposure down. Live \(p\) = —, so the — band is in force.

11 · Where the equations and the code part company

found while writing this page

Four places where a comment, a docstring or the methodology page says one thing and the running code does another. Each is stated here rather than quietly reconciled, because the point of this page is that it can be checked.

  • The partial adjustment in §9 is display-only; the recorded book is the full target.
  • The rebalance test prices a trade at 8bp of one-way turnover; the 10bp figure quoted elsewhere is what the live track record is charged, and applies to nothing else.
  • The minimum-variance feasibility check in §8 ignores the cluster caps, so a band it calls feasible can be infeasible once they bind.
  • A "view shift" trigger exists in the rebalance code and is never given a value, so it never fires.
