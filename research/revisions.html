How to read this

These are not forecasts and they are not recommendations. Each revision is the portfolio a mean-variance optimizer chooses given a set of expected returns, a covariance estimated on three years of daily data, and a stated constraint set. Change any of those three and the answer changes.

The candidates' expected returns are the weakest input on the page. They are not discounted cash flows — the screen that produced these names deliberately does not project. Each view is the company's normalised equity earnings yield: this year's revenue at its own five-year median EBIT margin, less interest, taxed, over market capitalisation. That is a no-growth perpetuity return, and it assumes a margin returns to a median that nothing guarantees. The Views halved revision exists precisely because that assumption deserves a stress test: if a satellite still earns its place with every candidate view cut in half, it is a position; if it does not, it was a forecast.

Volatility is the other thing to watch. These candidates carry two to three times the book's volatility, so a mean-variance optimizer inside a 19–22% band can only fund them by selling something safer. That is why the revision with a wider band is on the page: it separates "is this name worth owning" from "is there room for it".
