How the books are run

Methodology

Every step from a list of companies to an order, with the real parameter values, the papers each step rests on, and — at the end — everything this does not claim. Two books: one run by the engine described here, one run by hand to a rule stated in section 9.

1 · What this is

A long-only equity book, held in two brokerage accounts, and a set of programs that decide what it should hold. Nothing here places an order. The engine produces a target and a list of suggested changes with the evidence for each; a human reads them and trades.

The chain is: a hand-picked universe → a ten-year discounted cash-flow model per company → implied returns → Black-Litterman views against an equilibrium prior → a shrunk covariance with a forecast diagonal → a constrained optimizer → a trigger that decides whether the difference is worth trading → a partial move toward it. A regime nowcast scales the risk budget, a stress test applies four historical crashes to today's weights, and an options overlay is sized against the held book's beta.

The system is scaled for a $100,000 book. Real dollar values are never published.

2 · Data

four sources, one rule each

The universe

universe.py is one dict per company and the role field decides its fate: — names the optimizer may hold, — on the radar (priced and valued, never held), — valued for research only, and two benchmarks. A name added to the traded set seasons: it is scored but held out until it has 252 daily bars, so a name cannot enter the book on a window too short to estimate its covariance.

Prices

Three sources per name — the broker (IBKR), yfinance, and the vendor statement database — with identical schemas. The freshest file wins, provided it carries a comparable span of history; IBKR breaks ties because it is the execution venue. Both halves of that rule are scars. The unconditional-IBKR version let 17 names serve week-old prices after a successful backfill; the freshness-only version replaced 2.5 years of one name's history with a five-month stub from a different listing of the same company.

A staleness gate runs before every rebalance, per name and on the aligned panel, and aborts the run rather than solving on old prices. It exists because the daily price job reported success to the scheduler for 35 business days while every symbol silently failed.

Statements, macro, news

Company financials come from a vendor API into a local database; the loader computes no projections, only stores what was reported. Macro comes from FRED — 16 series back to 1990 — and every series carries a publication lag, so a reading is only visible on a date it could actually have been known. News is counted as a robust z-score against each name's own trailing baseline, with a publisher cap so one syndicated release is one piece of news, and a 20-day cool-off between a name appearing and any suggestion to buy it.

3 · Valuation, and how it becomes a view

The model

Each company gets a ten-year three-statement projection: five explicit years on stated assumptions, five fading to a steady state. Interest is solved to a fixed point (it depends on balances that depend on it), amortisation of acquired intangibles is separated from depreciation, and stock compensation is charged as a cash cost once. Every projected balance sheet ties — the test suite asserts the residual is under 1e-9 of total assets — because a projection that does not tie is hiding its error somewhere.

Value is the discounted free cash flow plus a value-driver terminal, TV = NOPAT(1 − g/RONIC)/(WACC − g), which makes growth cost capital: at a return on new capital equal to the cost of capital, growth adds nothing. Ten years rather than five because a five-year DCF leaves 65–85% of the value in the terminal.

From value to an expected return

The output that matters is not a price target. It is the implied return: the discount rate at which the model's value equals today's price, solved by root-find. Three of them, under a bear, base and bull case. That is a forward expected return an optimizer can use, and the spread between bear and bull is an honest statement of how sure the model is.

The prior

Views need something to disagree with. The prior is the equilibrium return — π = λ Σ wcap, what the market's own weights imply — with risk aversion set as λ = βuniverse × ERP / var(universe) rather than estimated from realised means. The textbook estimator implied returns of 55–69% a year for high-beta names and negative ones for low-beta names on this window: not merely imprecise, it inverted the cross-section. Both β and variance are second moments and estimate far better than a mean. Live: risk aversion — against a forward equity risk premium of —, the one number in the system asserted rather than estimated.

Black-Litterman

The posterior blends prior and views by their stated precisions. Views are in excess-return units to match the prior — mixing levels silently biases high-beta names — and each view's uncertainty is its own bear-to-bull spread, rescaled so the views and the prior get equal say (— confidence, τ = —). Before that rescaling the posterior correlated 0.93 with the views and 0.36 with the prior: the prior was decorative, and every weakness in a DCF passed straight through. It sits at equal weight because the scorecard that will measure whether these views predict has one archived run.

4 · Risk

Covariance is a statistical factor model, Σ = B F Bᵀ + D, on the trailing year of daily returns, with a Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage estimate computed alongside every run as a cross-check; if the two disagree on equal-weight volatility by more than 20%, the run says so.

Then the diagonal is replaced. Correlations are estimated long and volatility is forecast short, because they move at different speeds: correlation structure comes from the risk model, while each name's volatility comes from an EWMA forecast (λ = —, a half-life of about 11 days) or a GARCH(1,1) fit. Trailing one-year realised volatility is still published beside the forecast — purely so the change is auditable. It is never used as a fallback, because after a shock trailing vol stays elevated for a year by construction, which is exactly when the difference matters.

A complexity panel reports the ratio of names to observations, the Marchenko–Pastur eigenvalue edges and the realised shrinkage intensity. It is reported, never gating: the well-known result that Sharpe peaks at 10–15 holdings is evidence about unshrunk covariance, not an argument for a cap on holdings.

5 · Construction

The optimizer maximises expected return minus a risk penalty minus a turnover penalty, long-only and fully invested. The risk-aversion multiplier is not chosen — it is bisected until portfolio volatility lands inside the target band, so the band is the actual risk statement and the multiplier is its Lagrange multiplier.

Concentration by construction

Two passes. The first solves relaxed over every name; the second fixes the support that produced and re-solves with a 2% lower bound on every surviving name. Dust becomes impossible rather than something a cleanup heuristic chases afterwards — the greedy prune this replaced failed four distinct ways in a single afternoon. A cardinality floor of 8 names is imposed convexly, and the name cap is —, which is the same statement said twice: one over eight.

Caps that see the bet, not the label

Sector and theme budgets both apply, but the binding constraint is a cluster cap. A review found the book 65% in one exposure — hyperscale, AI compute and internet — with every one of those three inside its own 35% theme cap. A cap that only sees labels cannot see a bet that spans three of them. The AI complex cap is deliberately generous at 45%: it exists to stop 65%, not to forbid 45%.

Standing instructions

The owner overrides the model in specific, dated places: fixed weights, floors and exclusions. A fixed weight is a floor and a cap at the same number — when only the floor half was wired, one name fixed at 8.31% solved to 8.63%, and a fixed weight the optimizer may exceed is not an instruction, it is a suggestion. Every override is recorded with its reason and reported as an override, including the ones that raise the book's expected return.

The risk budget moves with the regime

The volatility band is — normally and — when the stress probability clears 50%. It scales exposure down in stress and never up in calm — the calm band is already the chosen risk appetite. The band's floor is a target rather than a minimum appetite: because the bisection stops at the first volatility inside the band, a wide band systematically under-spends the budget.

6 · When it actually trades

Solving a portfolio and trading a portfolio are different decisions. Before this layer existed, running the script was the rebalance — which on the record meant three rebalances in two months, twice within a day.

Four measurements on this book decide the design. Over a quarter the median largest name drifts 3.0% and the certainty-equivalent cost of that drift is 20bp; restoring it costs about 0.5bp at zero commissions. Cost is not what limits trading — estimation noise is. Re-drawing the same views within half their stated uncertainty and re-solving moves the target by 20.5% one-way turnover and changes three names. Noise moves the target three to six times more than a quarter of drift does. So the bands are sized against noise, and the move is partial.

Triggers

Hard triggers ignore the calendar entirely: the held book breaking a cap or a floor, holding a name that has been excluded, or volatility above the ceiling. Soft triggers — a name outside its band, a certainty-equivalent gain, a confirmed regime change — need 80 days since the last trade first.

A name is outside its band only if it is outside both an absolute 2pp and a relative 25%: a 25% relative move on a 1% position is 25bp and not worth a ticket, while a 2pp move on a 12.5% position is inside the noise.

The certainty-equivalent test

Is the new book actually better, net of cost? Score both books on the objective the optimizer maximises, using the investor's risk aversion. Using the optimizer's bisected multiplier instead — about 31.6 — makes the risk term roughly 13,000bp against a 319bp return term, which collapses the comparison into "which book has less variance" and once reported a 1,594bp loss for a trade whose entire content was the vol band doing its job. The gain must clear both twice the cost and a floor of 25bp, because twice-a-zero cost is trivially cleared by a rounding artefact.

The move is partial

When it does trade, the book moves 30% of the way to the target, and only in the names that were outside their bands. A noisy target deserves a partial response. The one exception is a policy breach — a name over its cap is brought inside the cap, because moving 30% of the way out of a breach still leaves a breach, and the hard trigger would simply fire again next run.

7 · Regime and stress

Two layers, because they are two different kinds of claim.

Layer A is estimated. A two-state Markov-switching model on twenty years of weekly index returns. Nothing about it is hand-set, and it finds weekly volatility of — calm against — stressed, with persistence around 0.97 and 0.92, identifying 2008, 2011, 2020 and 2022 on its own. Its probability is filtered, not smoothed: at each date it uses only data up to that date, because smoothed probabilities call the 2020 crash a week earlier than was knowable. Live reading: —.

Layer B is not estimated. Five narrative regimes — AI capex continues, AI digestion, inflation and energy shock, disinflation and cuts, recession — scored by a hand-written sheet over about a dozen conditions. It is an elicitation device, a consistent way to read conditions, and that is the entire claim made for it. It carries an entropy-based confidence floor, and below that floor the recommendation is explicitly to use the all-weather book rather than a regime one. Any action driven by Layer B is capped at low confidence in code.

The two cannot be merged: a recession and an inflation shock both look "stressed" and are opposite trades, so no amount of return data separates them.

Stress

A forecast covariance estimated on three calm years has never seen a crash. So today's weights are applied to the realised paths of four episodes — Lehman to the March 2009 trough, the COVID crash, the 2022 rate shock, and the August 2024 carry unwind — plus a daily VaR and conditional VaR on the actual holdings. Names too young to have been there borrow their cluster's sector ETF, and every borrowing is named in the output rather than silently substituted.

8 · The options overlay

and why it is currently close to a no-op

The volatility band de-risks by rotating equities on a quarterly cadence with a 30% partial adjustment: it takes months. A 2008 or 2020 move takes weeks. The overlay exists for that gap — index puts on one underlying, opened only when two of three conditions agree (stress probability, an inverted volatility term structure, a 5% drawdown from the 63-day high), with hysteresis on the way out so the boundary cannot whipsaw. Structure depends on where implied volatility sits in its own year: a plain put when it is cheap, a put spread when it is not, because after a spike the far tail is the most overpriced part of the surface.

Size comes from the held book's beta: enough delta to pull beta to 0.90, no more, so once the stressed vol band has already rotated the book lower the hedge shrinks by construction.

A long-put program has negative expected return. The case rests on convexity in those four episodes, on paying premium mostly when stress is likely, and on a hard budget cap of 150bp of NAV a year. A synthetic backtest from 2006 — options priced off the VIX term structure with a fixed skew, so it understates crash payoffs — says the conditioning is worth a great deal: hedging always costs about 18% a year in calm years and turns a +775% period into a loss, while the gate cuts that to about 2.4%. It also says the cap then buys most of the protection back: at 150bp the program funds about 8% of the contracts its own sizing calls for, delivering roughly 0.03 of the 0.40 beta reduction it targets, and its episode outcomes are within half a point of unhedged. That number is published rather than buried, because it is the finding that matters.

9 · How the CAD book is run

Nothing above applies to it. The CAD book holds — Canadian names — the big banks, an alternative lender, a pipeline, an asset manager and an equipment dealer — at equal weight, rebalanced by hand when drift makes a ticket worth paying for. Its target is equal weight over whatever is held, its bands are 5 percentage points on a name and 12% one-way across the book, and its benchmark is the S&P/TSX Composite.

No model has a view on these names: none is valued by the research stack, none is in the traded universe, and the engine never proposes a trade in them. What the engine does is measure the book — the same risk analytics, against its own benchmark. A book run by hand still deserves a stated target and an honest drift number; it just does not get an optimizer. The rule lives in books/cad.py so it can be read and tested, rather than in a chart.

10 · What this does not claim

the part worth reading twice

Two claims in this system have a way to be checked, and both are still accumulating evidence: a scorecard archives every run's expected returns and scores them against realised ones, and a point-in-time backtest tests the computable half of the earnings screen. The second has already returned a verdict — and the screen lost its veto because of it.
